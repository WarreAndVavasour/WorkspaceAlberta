# A place for “Wouldn't it be great if…”

The WorkspaceAlberta terminal is a physical space set up for a CEO to build the ideas they have been carrying around for years. Warre & Vavasour installs it, supports it, and works alongside the business to turn those ideas into useful, delivered work.

Think of the family doctor model of AI support. You have someone who gets to know your business, remembers what you have tried, helps work out what needs attention, and brings in the right specialist tools. You bring the ambition. We look after the moving parts and follow through with you.

### Your projects. Your Pi. Help close at hand.

WorkspaceAlberta is a network of devices connected for support over Tailscale. Each locally deployed Pi runs the workspaceAlberta harness, where your ideas and the little things you wish worked better become projects. You get to think out loud, try things and keep going without an engineer sitting in on every thought.

I work as the generalist: I know the business and its toolkit, and I show up when something needs attention. My practice is a small flock of about 20 distributed devices among trusted businesses. I think this is a new layer of work: helping businesses deploy, maintain and actually use AI tooling.

I stay connected to that workspace. When you get stuck, I come in and build with you on your device. WorkspaceAlberta keeps the plates spinning locally; I help with the bits that need an engineer. The Pi is a toolkit for a forward-deployed engineer, right where the work is happening.

Bring the small things too. The fiddly document you always wished you had. The awkward step nobody has had time to fix. The idea you never started. You don't need a polished pitch or a revenue case for every request. We build quickly, try it, iterate quickly and move on when something doesn't work. The 25% new-revenue arrangement belongs to the separate million-dollar challenge, not every project in your workspace.

Support access is configured with the company. Tailscale connects the devices; it does not make every device visible to every customer, guarantee a response time, or make cloud tools local. Access, availability and data handling are agreed during setup.

## What does the terminal do?

It starts with a real connection: the WorkspaceAlberta MCP server helps find public work through CanadaBuys and Alberta Purchasing Connection. Tell it what your company can do. Find opportunities, check the deadlines, read the requirements, and decide what is worth pursuing.

The owner keeps running the business. Bring your own “Wouldn't it be great if…” idea, including small things you always wanted fixed but never had time to start. A new product your company could sell. A pile of documents that could become a useful decision. A new service your team could deliver. Together we define the result, connect the systems you authorize, build something you can use, and check whether it helped. Each new connection and project is scoped with you; the procurement server does not arrive with access to all of your business systems.

The physical workspace gives this work a home. The harness carries the task through tools, files, documents and review. The support relationship gives it continuity. Your people bring the knowledge of the business. We want that knowledge doing more.

## One company. One million-dollar problem. Sixty days.

We're offering one installed and supported terminal to one selected Canadian company with a million-dollar problem worth solving. Any Canadian company can put an idea forward. We choose one problem together, agree what delivery means, and get to work.

**We have 60 days to find, fix, and deliver a solution. If we can't, we both walk away.** The terminal installation and support are provided without the monthly terminal fee for this 60-day engagement. The proposed commercial exchange is **25% of the attributable new revenue actually earned from the solution**, on terms agreed before the clock starts. There is no fee for an unrealized projection.

The share never applies to savings, headcount reductions, existing revenue or forecasts. We don’t cut corners. We don’t cut people. We build.

The point is to create economic activity you can point to: a delivered solution, work done, invoices paid, Canadian dollars earned. We each account for our own income and meet our applicable tax obligations. We want to earn our part by helping you earn yours.

Before starting, the written agreement identifies the baseline, the solution and acceptance test, the start and end dates, what counts as attributable new revenue earned, the collection period and payment schedule, refunds and costs, treatment of taxes, ownership of delivered work, confidentiality, and the equipment return or continuation arrangement. If delivery fails within 60 days, neither side owes a terminal or success fee under this offer. No one is promised a million dollars of revenue. Publication of the result needs the company's agreement.

## The work makes the tools better

I'm experimenting with how workspaceAlberta harness traces can help refine the tools: where a task stalled, which connection failed, what a person corrected, and what finally worked. First we use that evidence to improve the harness and evaluate changes. Further reinforcement-learning training is a research direction, not a promise that customer sessions automatically train a model.

Operational records, approved reusable skills and training datasets have different purposes. Customer material needs explicit permission and review before any reuse. A completed tool call is not a solved business problem; a solved problem is not money earned until the evidence says so. See [the learning and evaluation protocol](harness-learning.md).
